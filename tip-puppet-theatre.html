Never underestimate your neighbourhood
puppet theatre.

Small stage, serious craft, bar upstairs. The Théâtre des Marionnettes has been here this whole time and most people walk straight past it.

Tip by Dave

Plainpalais · Geneva

The Théâtre des Marionnettes de Genève sits on a side street in Plainpalais and is extremely easy to forget exists. Most people who live in Geneva have walked past it, registered it distantly, and thought they'd go some day. This is the tip to make you actually go.

The building gives you a clue — those red arched windows, the lettering above the door. It doesn't look like a tourist attraction, which is exactly right. It looks like somewhere that's been doing its thing since before you arrived, and will still be doing it after you leave. It has, and it will.

The bar

There's a café-bar upstairs. Get there early enough to have a drink before the show. The whole setup has the feel of a proper evening out rather than something you've dragged yourself to for the kids — which is partly the point. It's good for parents too.

The detail on these puppets is extraordinary. Chopin at the piano.
The show

The theatre is small, which turns out to be a feature. You're close to the stage, close enough to see the craft in the puppets themselves — the faces, the costumes, the articulation of the hands. The show we saw was performed entirely in French. It didn't matter at all. The language of puppeteering is its own thing.

"All in French, but the language of puppeteering knows no boundaries."

— Dave

The actors were completely invested. This is not a going-through-the-motions children's entertainment operation. These are people who take what they do seriously, and it shows in the performance. The kids in the audience were rapt. So were the adults.

Glowing jellyfish in the dark. The production values are legitimately impressive.
The verdict

Book it. Go before or after with a drink. Let yourself be surprised by how good it is. Geneva has a habit of hiding things worth knowing in plain sight, and the puppet theatre is a perfect example. It's been there all along.

The tip

Book ahead: Shows sell out, especially on weekends. Check the programme at marionnettes.ch.

Bar upstairs: Open before shows. Worth arriving early for.

Language: All in French, but the storytelling is visual enough that it doesn't matter for kids or non-French speakers.

Getting there: Tram 15 or 17 to Plainpalais, then a short walk. Rue Rodo 3.
