Want a Nomos in Geneva?
There is exactly one shop.

Bijouterie Grégoire on Rue Etienne-Dumont is the only stockist in town for Nomos Glashütte — the small German watchmaker quietly making some of the best Bauhaus-leaning timepieces around. The street alone is worth the walk.

The shop

Geneva is the global capital of Swiss watches. Patek, Vacheron, Rolex around the corner, Audemars an hour up the road. Walk into any boutique on Rue du Rhône and you'll see them all. What you won't see, anywhere, is a Nomos. Except here.

Bijouterie Grégoire is a small jeweller tucked into the Old Town, two arched windows, a cream g on the door. Ring the bell. Inside it's quiet, well-lit, and the Nomos display sits in a single case — seven or eight watches on hexagonal wooden blocks, with a couple of ceramic acorns thrown in for the Saxony reference. Not the full catalogue. Enough to see the line in person, try a few on, and stop guessing about case sizes.

Tue–Fri 11h–18h30. Sat 11h–17h30. Sonnez SVP.
About Nomos

Nomos is German, not Swiss. Based in Glashütte in Saxony — the same small town that gave us A. Lange & Söhne. The aesthetic is Bauhaus: clean dials, sans-serif numerals, no clutter. They make their own movements in-house, which almost no one in their price bracket does.

The full display. Acorns are a Saxony nod.

The line covers the obvious anchors — Tangente, Metro, Club, Ludwig, Orion — plus a few of the more playful pieces. Steel bracelets, leather, NATO straps. Quartz at the entry end, hand-wound and automatic going up.

Hand-lettered. The whole shop is like this.

"A German watch, in the global capital of Swiss watches. Slightly subversive. That's part of the fun."

— Dave
Why bother with Nomos

In-house movements at a fraction of what comparable Swiss makers charge. Bauhaus discipline. No logos shouting at you across a room.

Glashütte, Saxony. A small German watchmaking town with serious horological pedigree. Nomos has been there since 1990.

Try before you buy. Case sizes look very different on the wrist than online. Worth twenty minutes here before you order anything.

The walk to it

Rue Etienne-Dumont sits just below the Cathédrale Saint-Pierre at the eastern edge of the Old Town. Cobblestones, arched ground floors, wrought-iron balconies, the kind of quiet you don't get on the lakefront. Combine the visit with a wander up to the cathedral and a coffee on Place du Bourg-de-Four — the prettiest square in Geneva and a two-minute walk away.

Looking back up the street. Cathedral spire on the right.
The verdict

If you live in Geneva and want a serious mechanical watch that doesn't cost what a small car costs, Nomos is the obvious answer — and Bijouterie Grégoire is the only place in town to see one in the flesh. Even if you're not buying, walk in. Then walk back up the street toward the cathedral. The trip is worth it either way.
