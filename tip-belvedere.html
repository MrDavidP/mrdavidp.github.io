HoodTip / Dave / Wengen, Switzerland
Hotel · Spa · Restaurant · Wengen, Switzerland

Go to Wengen. Stay at the Belvedere.
Stop searching.

A newly renovated grand hotel in a car-free Swiss alpine village you reach by cog railway. The wagyu bresaola will stop you cold. The outdoor pool in the snow will finish the job.

Tip by Dave
·
Grand Hotel Belvedere
·
Galliweidli, 3823 Wengen BE
·
Bernese Oberland · Switzerland
Getting there

The first thing Wengen does is remove you, from your car. Park in Lauterbrunnen at 800 metres. Board the Wengernalpbahn — running since 1893 — and ride up through forest and snow to the village at 1,274 metres. No cars. No traffic. The streets are quiet. Twenty minutes on a cog railway and something has already shifted.

The Belvedere sends a small electric vehicle to collect you at the station. Slightly Wes Anderson. By the time you arrive, you've forgotten whatever you were stressed about.

Getting there

From Geneva: Intercity to Interlaken Ost, Berner Oberland Bahn to Lauterbrunnen, Wengernalpbahn to Wengen. About 2.5 hours. Leave the car at Lauterbrunnen station.

The line: Opened 1893. Continues up to Kleine Scheidegg at 2,061m, connecting to the Jungfraubahn — the highest railway station in Europe at 3,454m.

Green shutters. Belle Époque bones. Concrete spa wing around back.
The hotel

Old bones, smart renovation. Arched windows, carved balconies, ornamental ironwork. Tyler Brûlé was involved in some capacity and you can feel it — the wallpaper in the bar, the way the spaces are edited rather than stuffed. It doesn't feel preserved. It feels like somewhere that knows what it is.

Two chairs. A bottle. The Jungfrau through the window.
The food

Switzerland is not a food country. It is bread, cheese, keep moving. My expectations were low. Then the wagyu bresaola arrived.

I stopped talking mid-sentence. Thin-sliced, soft, fatty, deeply savoury. I thought about it for the rest of the trip. Every meal after was excellent. Eat here. Maybe one pizza in the village to walk around. Come back for everything else.

The bar. Dark floral wallpaper, chandelier, long mirror.
22 CHF. Worth it.

"The cocktail guy works Scorpios in Mykonos in summer. Smoky negroni, small decanter, large cube. 22 CHF. Pay the money."

— Dave

The sommelier is also Greek, serious about wine. He poured from Germany and the bottle was so good we ordered a case sent home. Didn't expect to leave Wengen with German wine. It happened.

"The dessert wine was a bit strong. Adding fresh snow helped."

— Dave
The rooms

The rooms face the valley. Clear day: Jungfrau at 4,158 metres, the church steeple of Wengen, the Lauterbrunnen valley below. Cloudy day: snow-covered pines disappearing into white. Both are fine.

The balcony. Valley at 800m below.
The Jungfrau, 4,158m.
Wengen after overnight snow.
The spa

Not a skiing person. There's a small train further up the mountain, you can ski back down to the village. I watched others do it. The spa is where I was. Sauna and steam both excellent — very hot, clean. Cold plunge, good showers, pool. The spa wing is new poured concrete bolted to the 19th-century hotel, and the contrast works. My wife rated the massage A+. She is a picky judge.

Heated. Outside. In the snow.
Indoor pool. Brutalism and warm water.
Original oil. You actually look at it.
The verdict

Someone with real taste came through here and made decisions. Skip the skiing. Use the spa. Eat the wagyu bresaola. Sit in the lobby with a glass of something and look at the mountains. That's a complete holiday. Book it.
