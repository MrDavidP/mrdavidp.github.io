Home to international organisations, banks, and watches — and the people behind them. The French Alps in the distance, vineyards on the hillsides, the lake running through the middle. Switzerland's francophone outpost feels quietly utopian.

It's also home to generations of Portuguese, Italian, and other Europeans who came to build their lives here. They brought good food, a decent pub culture, and the sense that Geneva is a real city, not just an institution. Find your neighbourhood, find your market. This guide will help.

Good to know
🚌
Transport
Staying in a hotel? You get a free Geneva Transport Card — buses, trams, trains, boats. If you live here, the yearly UNIRESO pass is worth it. Under 25? Public transport is free in the canton since 2025.
🇫🇷
France is 10 minutes away
Groceries in France cost roughly 30% less. Annemasse, Saint-Julien, Ferney-Voltaire — all easy by car or bike. Most Geneva residents do a weekly or monthly run. Worth knowing early.
🗣️
French — but English works
Geneva is the most international city in Switzerland. English is widely spoken at restaurants, shops, and services. That said, making an effort in French goes a long way. Bonjour before anything else.
☀️
Seasons matter
Summer: La Barge, Bains des Pâquis, swimming in the Rhône. Winter: fog (la grisaille), fondue, ski weekends an hour away. Spring: the best time — lake swims start in May, market season opens.
11 Neighbourhoods
01 Carouge What Geneva would be if it relaxed. Italian architecture, Saturday market, natural wine, oysters. The best neighbourhood in the city. 4 tips
02 Saint-Gervais Best Guinness. Best grocery. Bongo Joe on the Rhône. La Barge in summer. The neighbourhood closest to the station with the most going on. 5 tips
03 Plainpalais Students, artists, the big red plain. Victoria Hall, smashburgers, sushi, pizza. Less polished than the rest of Geneva — in a good way. 4 tips
04 Pâquis Geneva's most international neighbourhood, right on the lake. The bains at sunset. The best sushi in the city. Everything stays open late. 2 tips
05 Eaux-Vives The quay, Parc La Grange, vintage posters. Calmer than Plainpalais, less touristy than Rive. A neighbourhood locals actually live in. 3 tips
06 Nations The UN, the Red Cross, half the world's NGOs — and shockingly few good places to eat. The ones that exist are worth knowing about. 3 tips
07 Rive JLC on Rue du Rhône. Ottolenghi at the Mandarin. Proper city centre, proper city prices. Worth a specific trip for specific things. 2 tips
08 Vieille Ville The old town. Cobblestones, the cathedral, Bastions park. The library is free and the NOMOS dealer is on Rue de la Cité. 2 tips
09 Cornavin The main station. Not a destination in itself, but one tip worth knowing before you board any intercity train in Switzerland. 1 tip
10 Champel Quiet, elevated, residential. Doctors, diplomats, good cheese shops. Coming soon
11 St-Jean Along the Rhône to the west. Getting more interesting. Coming soon
